## Executive Decision

LotteryCorner should not launch a traditional forum that forces users to first understand categories, subforums and posting rules.

It should create:

> **A lottery conversation layer available from every relevant page, with one simple posting experience and one canonical discussion object.**

The Community product must preserve what makes LotteryPost engaging:

- game-specific communities;
- state-specific familiarity;
- long-running daily and monthly threads;
- systems and number discussions;
- winner and ticket stories;
- opinion, superstition and mathematics living together;
- recurring contributors who become recognizable;
- active-topic discovery;
- simple human language;
- low formality.

LotteryCorner should improve the experience through:

- posting from many entry points;
- automatic game/state/draw/article context;
- AI-assisted title and category selection;
- question-first posting;
- photo and number-set support;
- direct links to results, tools and archives;
- one labelled LotteryCorner AI research reply where helpful;
- strong mobile usability;
- better duplicate control;
- clear moderation, privacy and Responsible Play rules;
- structured data and search visibility for useful public discussions.

The user should not have to “go to the forum” before asking a question.

They should be able to post from wherever the question arises.

# PART I — WHAT LOTTERY COMMUNITIES ACTUALLY DISCUSS

## 1. LotteryPost Engagement Concentration

LotteryPost’s visible forum taxonomy and activity demonstrate where sustained lottery discussion happens.

As of the research date, its public forum page showed:

- Pick 3: more than 142,000 topics and more than 5 million posts;
- Pick 4: nearly 40,000 topics and more than 1.1 million posts;
- Pick 5: more than 6,800 topics and more than 137,000 posts;
- Jackpot Games: more than 15,000 topics and more than 294,000 posts;
- Lottery Systems: nearly 9,000 topics and nearly 299,000 posts;
- General Lottery Discussion: nearly 28,000 topics and more than 405,000 posts;
- Mystical Discussion: more than 34,000 topics and more than 341,000 posts;
- Mathematics: more than 1,400 topics and more than 24,000 posts;
- Scratch-Off Games: more than 2,000 topics and more than 49,000 posts;
- Lottery News: more than 9,600 stories and more than 185,000 comments.

### Strategic conclusion

The heaviest repeat engagement is not concentrated only around Powerball and Mega Millions.

It is concentrated around:

1. daily games;
2. repeated posting habits;
3. number sharing;
4. methods and systems;
5. state/game-specific familiarity;
6. ongoing contributor relationships.

LotteryCorner must therefore treat Pick 3, Pick 4 and similar daily-game communities as first-class products—not lower-priority subforums beneath jackpot news.

## 2. Daily Number-Game Conversation

Common topics include:

- today’s numbers;
- midday versus evening;
- state-specific picks;
- pairs;
- sums;
- roots;
- doubles and triples;
- hot/cold numbers;
- repeats;
- positional digits;
- workouts;
- followers posting selections;
- win reports;
- “what are you playing?”;
- monthly or weekly tracking threads;
- member-created charts;
- system performance;
- asking another contributor to explain a method.

### Product implication

Daily-game communities need:

- persistent monthly threads;
- structured number-entry helpers;
- draw-specific context;
- easy copy/reuse of number sets;
- result matching after each draw;
- optional automatic update showing whether posted selections matched;
- contributor follow controls;
- game/state filters;
- pinned explanation of thread terminology;
- links from Game and Year Archive pages.

## 3. Jackpot-Game Conversation

Common themes:

- jackpot size;
- cash versus annuity;
- taxes;
- anonymity;
- winner location;
- Quick Pick versus own numbers;
- number selection;
- office/group pools;
- “what would you do?”;
- game price and rule changes;
- Power Play and Double Play;
- claim deadlines;
- whether a winner has come forward;
- reactions to news;
- saved-number experiences.

### Product implication

Powerball and Mega Millions discussion should connect directly to:

- current result;
- latest drawing;
- jackpot;
- tax and annuity tools;
- state offering page;
- related news;
- yearly archive;
- saved numbers;
- Buy Tickets where contextually permitted.

## 4. Systems and Strategy

LotteryPost sustains separate conversations around:

- systems;
- wheels;
- filtering;
- positional analysis;
- pairs and combinations;
- charts;
- mathematical methods;
- backtesting;
- software/tools;
- number generators;
- user-created approaches.

### Product implication

LotteryCorner should support a structured **System Post** type with:

- system name;
- target game;
- explanation;
- number set or rule;
- date range;
- tool link;
- backtest link;
- assumptions;
- attachments/images;
- update log;
- community replies.

The system is presented as a member method or research workflow—not as a guaranteed-win claim.

## 5. Mathematics, Probability and Data

Smaller than Pick 3/Pick 4 but strategically important:

- probability;
- combinations;
- odds;
- randomness;
- expected value;
- draw distributions;
- statistical interpretation;
- chart methodology;
- data quality.

### Product implication

This is where LotteryCorner AI and deterministic tools can add high-value explanations while preserving accessible language.

The platform should separate:

- mathematical fact;
- historical observation;
- member theory;
- AI explanation;
- personal belief.

## 6. Mystical and Superstitious Discussion

LotteryPost’s Mystical forum is visibly large.

Themes include:

- dreams;
- meaningful dates;
- numerology;
- astrology;
- signs;
- visualization;
- intuition;
- spiritual interpretation.

### Product implication

LotteryCorner should not eliminate this persona merely because it is not scientific.

It should provide a clearly labelled community area such as:

> **Dreams, Signs and Lucky Numbers**

Rules:

- clearly community belief/personal interpretation;
- no official or scientific claim;
- no guaranteed-win language;
- no paid psychic/scam solicitation;
- Responsible Play reminders;
- AI should not validate supernatural claims as fact.

This respects the actual lottery-player persona rather than designing only for technically minded users.

## 7. Scratch-Off and Winner Stories

Reddit’s lottery community visibly emphasizes:

- ticket photos;
- scratcher wins;
- personal celebration;
- questions about symbols or prize interpretation;
- retailer/state labels;
- misprint and dispute stories;
- “finally won” posts;
- repeated small-win sharing;
- concerns about problematic play.

### Product implication

LotteryCorner should support a visual **Share a Win / Ticket Story** flow with:

- photo upload;
- automatic sensitive-data redaction;
- state;
- game;
- amount;
- short story;
- optional retailer;
- verified/unverified label;
- comments;
- easy sharing;
- Responsible Play support where language indicates harm.

Ticket images must automatically detect or require removal of:

- barcode;
- serial number;
- claim number;
- personal information.

## 8. Questions and Help

Common questions include:

- “Did I win?”
- “How do I claim?”
- “Can I remain anonymous?”
- “What does this ticket symbol mean?”
- “Why was this prize different?”
- “What time is the drawing?”
- “Can I buy online?”
- “What is boxed/straight?”
- “How does Fireball work?”
- “Can someone explain this system?”
- “Has anyone experienced this?”

### Product implication

Questions should be the easiest post type.

A user should be able to type one sentence from any page and submit after:

1. automatic context detection;
2. suggested duplicate answers;
3. optional AI first response;
4. sign-in at the final submit step where required.

# PART II — LOTTERY PLAYER PERSONA

## 9. Design for Familiarity, Not Technical Sophistication

The core persona is not a software engineer or professional statistician.

The interface should use:

- familiar game names;
- state names;
- plain actions;
- short forms;
- visible examples;
- number keyboards;
- photo-first posting;
- conversational wording;
- minimal configuration.

Avoid forcing users to understand:

- taxonomy;
- game IDs;
- content types;
- tags;
- markdown;
- schema;
- advanced statistics;
- moderation terminology.

Behind the interface, LotteryCorner may infer all of these.

## 10. Recommended Posting Language

Use:

- Ask a Question
- Share Numbers
- Share a Win
- Start a Discussion
- Explain a System
- Create a Poll
- Reply
- Follow
- Save
- Add Photo

Avoid using “Create Thread” as the only primary action, although experienced forum users may still recognize it in secondary language.

## 11. Emotional Motivations

People participate for:

- hope;
- recognition;
- routine;
- belonging;
- celebration;
- validation;
- curiosity;
- teaching;
- rivalry between methods;
- state/community identity;
- storytelling;
- being helpful.

The Community design must support these motivations without encouraging excessive play or false certainty.

# PART IV — UNIVERSAL COMPOSER

## 24. Step 1 — One Prompt

Default field:

```text
What do you want to ask or share?
```

The user may type plain language.

## 25. Step 2 — Automatic Understanding

The system suggests:

- post type;
- game;
- state;
- draw;
- category;
- title;
- possible duplicate discussions.

Example:

User types:

> Anyone playing 317 in Florida Pick 3 evening tonight?

System infers:

```text
Post type: Share Numbers / Discussion
State: Florida
Game: Pick 3 Evening
Draw: Next scheduled evening draw
Numbers: 317
```

The user can correct the suggestions.

## 26. Step 3 — Optional Structured Helpers

Based on inferred type:

### Question

- title;
- detail;
- optional photo;
- “What have you tried?” optional;
- answer-needed label.

### Share Numbers

- number set;
- draw;
- play type;
- short note;
- public/private confirmation.

### Share a Win

- photo;
- game;
- state;
- amount;
- story;
- sensitive-data check.

### System

- method;
- game;
- rule;
- example;
- tool/backtest;
- disclaimer.

### Poll

- question;
- 2–6 choices;
- closing date;
- neutral wording.

## 27. Step 4 — Duplicate Prevention

Before submission, show:

- similar active question;
- current monthly game thread;
- related article discussion;
- existing answer or guide.

Actions:

- join existing discussion;
- continue posting anyway;
- revise question.

Do not block a legitimate new angle automatically.

## 28. Step 5 — AI Assistance

Optional:

- improve title;
- clarify wording;
- add game/state context;
- detect private data;
- flag guaranteed-win language;
- suggest relevant result/tool;
- offer immediate factual answer.

The user retains ownership of the post.

AI does not rewrite the user into technical language.

## 29. Step 6 — Sign-In Timing

Anonymous visitor may:

- type the complete question;
- see detected context;
- see duplicate suggestions;
- receive one useful AI answer where appropriate.

Sign-in is requested to:

- publish;
- upload media;
- follow replies;
- save number sets;
- participate in polls.

The user’s entered content is preserved through sign-in.

## 30. Step 7 — Publish

Show:

- final title;
- community destination;
- privacy state;
- attached context;
- AI label;
- moderation warning only when necessary.

One tap publishes.

The AI should not pretend to “take time” artificially. The system may post after completing a governed research workflow.

# PART VII — AI ROLE

## 46. AI First Answer

The founder’s desired differentiation is:

> A user can ask the community, and LotteryCorner AI can provide the first useful answer while humans build on it.

Recommended behavior:

1. User posts a question.
2. Duplicate/guide answer appears immediately if available.
3. Deterministic or simple factual AI answer may appear quickly.
4. Complex question becomes a labelled LotteryCorner Research answer.
5. Humans reply, correct, add experience and debate.
6. AI may update a current-fact banner if facts change.

## 47. AI Does Not Dominate

AI replies only when:

- the author asks;
- the question needs a factual first answer;
- no human answer exists after a configured period;
- a moderator requests research;
- misinformation creates safety risk;
- a thread summary is useful after sufficient activity.

AI does not reply to every opinion or every number-sharing post.

## 48. AI Thread Summary

Show after sufficient real activity.

Possible blocks:

- What people agree on;
- Different viewpoints;
- Facts verified;
- Questions still open;
- Useful links.

Do not manufacture consensus.

## 49. AI and Member Language

AI should answer in plain player language.

It may define technical terms when needed, but should not rewrite the community into an academic or software-product tone.

## 50. AI Transparency

Every AI post shows:

- LotteryCorner AI or LotteryCorner Research identity;
- generation/update time;
- sources/data;
- correction status;
- whether a tool was used.

# PART IX — MODERATION, SAFETY AND TRUST

## 55. Moderation Categories

- harassment;
- hate;
- personal data;
- spam;
- affiliate solicitation;
- fake winning claim;
- ticket selling;
- scam;
- guaranteed-win claim;
- dangerous financial advice;
- loss-chasing encouragement;
- impersonation;
- stolen content;
- copyright issue;
- irrelevant sports betting;
- underage participation;
- self-harm or distress.

## 56. Ticket and Winner Privacy

Automatically or manually redact:

- barcode;
- serial;
- claim number;
- QR code;
- exact personal address;
- phone/email;
- account information.

Warn before publishing.

## 57. Scam and Solicitation

Prohibit:

- paid number sellers;
- private-message “guarantees”;
- claim-fee scams;
- fake officials;
- payment requests;
- account/ticket purchases;
- unapproved affiliate links.

## 58. Responsible Play

Detect language indicating:

- chasing losses;
- spending beyond means;
- distress;
- compulsion;
- borrowing to play.

Respond with:

- non-judgmental support;
- limit tools;
- quiet options;
- professional help links;
- reduced promotional prompts.

Do not publicly shame the user.

## 59. Mathematics versus Belief

Allow both when clearly labelled.

The platform distinguishes:

- Verified Fact;
- Historical Data;
- Member Theory;
- Personal Belief;
- LotteryCorner AI Explanation.

## 60. Appeals and Enforcement

Actions:

```text
CONTENT_WARNING
LIMIT_REACH
REMOVE_MEDIA
REMOVE_POST
TEMPORARY_MUTE
TEMPORARY_SUSPENSION
PERMANENT_BAN
REQUIRE_REVIEW
```

Provide:

- reason;
- policy;
- appeal route;
- moderator audit log;
- restoration where approved.

# PART XI — SEO AND STRUCTURED DATA

## 66. Public versus Private

Indexable candidates:

- substantial public discussion;
- useful Q&A;
- system explanation;
- recurring game thread with meaningful content;
- verified community story;
- public member profile with sufficient activity.

Noindex/private:

- empty thread;
- one-line low-value post;
- duplicate;
- removed/moderated content;
- private group;
- sensitive claim/ticket issue;
- account support;
- thin auto-created recurring thread.

## 67. Discussion Schema

Google’s current guidance states that `DiscussionForumPosting` is for user-generated posts and should not be used for publisher-authored articles, even when an article has comments.

Use for a genuine community thread:

```text
DiscussionForumPosting
```

Possible nested types:

```text
Comment
Person
InteractionCounter
ImageObject
VideoObject
BreadcrumbList
WebPage
```

Required and recommended fields must match visible content.

The full original post and visible comments included in markup must reflect the text actually present on the page.

## 68. Q&A Schema

If a page is primarily a user question with answers, evaluate current Google Q&A requirements during implementation.

Do not use Q&A markup on:

- open-ended discussion;
- publisher article;
- poll;
- number-share thread;
- system journal;
- news article.

## 69. Profile Schema

Community profile:

```text
ProfilePage
mainEntity: Person
```

Use:

- name or alternateName;
- image only when genuine and visible;
- description;
- identifier;
- interaction statistics only when accurate;
- sameAs only for user-provided verified external profiles.

## 70. Community Home Schema

Conceptual:

```text
CollectionPage
BreadcrumbList
ItemList
```

ItemList represents only visible threads/categories.

## 71. Canonical and Pagination

- one canonical route per thread;
- crawlable pagination or accessible incremental loading;
- canonical thread URL preserved;
- reply anchors;
- no indexable sort/filter explosion;
- deleted content returns the correct status or transparent removed state;
- merged duplicate redirects to the surviving thread.

## 72. Server-Visible Content

Initial HTML includes:

- title;
- root post;
- author/profile;
- date;
- context entities;
- visible replies or crawlable pages;
- accepted/helpful answer where relevant;
- source/tool links;
- moderation/correction state.

Do not make the thread’s unique content dependent only on client-side AI.
